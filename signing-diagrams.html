The Signing Chain

Signing a file, a CSR, and a certificate are the same hash-and-sign trick wearing three different shapes — and an mTLS handshake is where one of those private keys finally gets used live, not just once at issuance.

1 — Three ways to sign

Same pipeline every time: hash the data, then encrypt that hash with a private key. What changes is whose private key signs, and whether the signature ends up beside the data or embedded inside it.

2 — mTLS: the private key, live

The CA is not on the call. It already finished its job offline, once, when it signed the CSR. During the actual connection, the TLS server checks the client — using a copy of the CA's certificate it already has in its trust store.
